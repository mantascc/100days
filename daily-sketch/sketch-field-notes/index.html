Field notes
on a house style
that was not designed.

Sixty-nine wrote it down. Seventy laid it out. Neither is honest about how it got there — the aesthetic fell out of ninety sketches, and the shape it takes today is a shape it can lose tomorrow.

observed across 89 sketches
6 principles, 3 exceptions

specimen strip · live ↳ ambient

A design system says this is what we do. A moodboard says this is what I like. Neither is quite right for what happened here. What happened here is closer to what keeps happening.

Between the two: field notes. The kind you write watching birds. You do not declare a plumage; you note that the same one keeps showing up, from which distance, in what light, next to what other birds — and you leave room for the ones you cannot classify yet.

So this page treats the sketchbook as the field. Each recurring move is a sighting, cited to the sketches it appeared in. Each rule is followed by the exception that broke it and worked anyway. And each open question — the ones a real design system would close — stays open, because that is where the next sketch comes from.

§03live plate · owned moves

Four gestures the sketchbook has learned by heart.

These are not tokens. They are the small techniques that keep coming back — the muscle memory of the practice. Each panel below runs the actual move, at low intensity, and cites the sketches where it was learned.

M01
proximity link
Draw a line between two agents under a distance threshold, faded by separation. Turns a cloud into a mesh.
↳ 07 · 09 · 10 · 11 · 80
M02
glyph density ramp
Continuous field quantised across an ordered character set. Sparse to dense · brightness becomes character.
↳ 53 · 55 · 66 · 67 · 77 · 78
M03
summed sines drift
Three or four sines out of phase, summed, sampled on a grid. Pseudo-noise that stays legible as sine.
↳ 12 · 39 · 51 · 68
M04
per-cell threshold
Each grid cell holds a value and flips at its own threshold. Rules stay local · a pattern emerges.
↳ 18 · 40 · 52
Overfamiliar makes a move a fingerprint · and a trap. Proximity-link is the sketchbook's signature; anything drawn through it looks "generative" instantly. A candidate for the /spark break strategy — see §06.
§04tensions · kept open

The questions the practice refuses to close.

A design system's job is to answer questions. This one's job is to keep four of them un-answered. Each has been circled repeatedly and never quite resolved — which is the point. They are the pressure that makes the next sketch necessary.

tension · 01charge: high
Can randomness read as choice?
Uniform noise feels like noise. A flat mapping is a gradient, not an exploration. The working answer so far is piecewise, phase-banded — but "authored randomness" stays a paradox worth keeping open: lean too far and you've just hidden a decision behind a dice.
↳ pressed in 01 · 04 · 07 · 56
tension · 02charge: medium
Do you show the mechanism, or polish the surface?
An early maxim — "show the mechanism, don't hide it; polish is reduction" — pulls against the later sketches' bloom, glass, and gradients. The two impulses aren't enemies, but they want opposite things: the raw piece that teaches its own logic vs. the resolved object that hides its seams.
↳ pressed in 43 · 47 · 81
tension · 03charge: medium
Is a sketchbook a search or a map?
First half searched — one-off pieces looking for a language. Second half maps — reference sheets, palettes, catalogues, indexed thumbnails. The design-system impulse can crowd out the wilder veins if left unchecked. Kept open so the mapping doesn't seal.
↳ pressed in 43 · 57 · 61 · 71 · this page
tension · 04charge: medium
Iterating a system or writing new rules?
Every sketch either refines a move already owned (proximity link, glyph ramp) or invents a new mechanism from scratch. Both are legitimate. Neither should be the default. The tension is what stops iteration from becoming autopilot.
↳ pressed across every second sketch
§05exceptions · logged

Three times the rule broke and it worked.

The system is defined as much by its exceptions as by its rules. When a sketch violates a principle and the result holds up, the exception is not filed as damage — it is filed as pressure to rewrite. Two of these are already pushing on their principles. One more and reduction-as-polish should grow an explicit "effect-as-material" clause.

28-liquid-glassverdict: worked
Broke reduction-as-polish. A draggable frosted pane stacks backdrop-filter blur, saturate, brightness, contrast, and an SVG feTurbulence → feDisplacementMap liquid distortion. Pure additive richness.
Because the effect is the subject. The sketch is a glass simulator; glass that does not blur, saturate, and warp is not glass. The richness is not decoration applied to content — it is the content. Reduction here would have deleted the entire idea, not polished it.
↳ against principles/reduction-as-polish
63-ube-kaizenverdict: mixed
Broke one accent per project. Ube Kaizen runs three named colors at once — violet, lavender, terra — as a harmonized triad, plus pulsing accent demos. More accent presence than the single-spectral rule allows.
Mostly. The sketch is a color tool — a single accent would defeat the premise. The triad stays quiet because the three are desaturated and harmonized. Suggests the rule is about perceived loudness, not literal accent count. A muted multi-accent can be quieter than one neon single.
↳ against principles/quiet-over-loud
81-lens-waveverdict: worked
Same rule, deeper violation. A WebGL shader adds three-ring bloom, chromatic aberration splitting R/G/B, sine-wave warp, non-uniform lens scale. Glow, fringe, gradient-tinted light — three named anti-patterns at once.
The bloom and aberration read as optics, not styling: the text behaves like a luminous material seen through a lens, so the fringe reads as a consequence of physics rather than a chosen color. Second logged break in the same direction. Two exceptions is the recurrence threshold.
↳ against principles/reduction-as-polish · pressure to rewrite
§06not yet · anti-echo

What the sketchbook hasn't done — on purpose.

Every strong practice risks becoming its own echo chamber. These prompts come from the /spark break strategy — the anti-echo move that surfaces the sketch you have been avoiding. They are what the sketchbook is missing, phrased as invitations.

01 break · vein A sketch with no emergence — a single, closed, authored image with the mechanism made deliberately illegible. The opposite of every rule-and-let-it-run piece so far. ↳ vs veins/emergence-from-local-rules
02 break · move A network sketch that refuses the proximity-link mesh. Same particle cloud, different fingerprint — force, gradient, sound, memory. Anything but a faded line under a threshold. ↳ vs moves/proximity-link-drawing
03 press · tension Take a glassy, finished sketch and strip it back to its raw mechanism · a diptych, polished on one half, exposed on the other. Split the tension in half and show both. ↳ presses tensions/showing-mechanism-vs-polish
04 collide A reference sheet that generates itself from a single rule — a taxonomy that self-organises. Design-system meets emergence: the map is the search. ↳ collides veins/design-system × veins/emergence
